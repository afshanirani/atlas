# Template: Spec / PRD Outline

Skeleton for `spec-draft`. Adjust to your organization's expected format — the value is
in having *a* consistent structure, not this particular one.

`last_verified: TODO`

---

## Summary
One paragraph. What is changing and for whom. Readable by someone with no context.

## Problem
The user or business problem, with evidence. What happens if nothing is built.

## Goals & non-goals
Explicit non-goals prevent the scope conversation from recurring every review.

## Success measures
How anyone knows this worked. Baseline, target, and how it's measured.

## Users & use cases
Who this serves and the specific situations it addresses.

## Requirements
What the solution must do. Separate must-have from should-have honestly.

## Open questions
**Keep this section prominent.** In a first draft it is the most useful part — an honest
list of unknowns beats plausible filler.

## Dependencies & risks
What this needs from others, and what could go wrong.

## Rollout
Phasing, gating, and what happens if it needs rolling back.

## Decisions log
Decision, date, rationale, and options rejected. Written as you go, not afterward.

---

## Drafting rules

- Mark unknowns explicitly rather than generating plausible content
- Pull real context from `context/` — stakeholders, related initiatives, prior decisions
- A first draft's job is to surface the right questions, not to look finished
